GCC High Platform Solutioning

Employee Engagement · Internal Communications · M365 Architecture

The Problem

Your GCC High Tenant Is Probably Running at 20% of Its Capability

Most defense contractors operating in GCC High got there because a contract required it. The migration happened, the licenses are paid, and Teams works for calls. But the platform layer — the employee experience, internal communications architecture, and governance model — was never built out. That gap has real consequences.

Employees work around the tenant. Files end up in personal OneDrive accounts or shared via email because SharePoint was never configured correctly. Internal communications happen in Teams channels nobody monitors, or worse, in tools outside the GCC High boundary entirely. Meanwhile, the retention policies, DLP rules, and sensitivity label taxonomy that your CMMC assessor will ask about either don't exist or were set up years ago and haven't been touched since.

GCC High is a capable platform. Teams, SharePoint, Viva Engage, Viva Connections, Power Platform — all available, all usable within a CMMC boundary. The challenge is that GCC High differs from commercial Microsoft 365 in ways that matter: feature availability lags, Graph API endpoints differ, guest access is more restricted, and some integrations that work in commercial simply don't exist in GCC High. Building the right platform strategy requires knowing those differences before you commit to an architecture, not after.

We have deployed and managed M365 in a GCC High environment under active DCSA scrutiny. We know what the platform can do, where it falls short of commercial, and how to configure it so your employees actually use it — without creating compliance gaps in the process.

Common Questions

GCC High Platform FAQ

How is GCC High different from commercial Microsoft 365 for employee engagement and communications?
GCC High carries FedRAMP High authorization and US data residency requirements, which means some commercial Microsoft 365 features are unavailable or lag commercial release by months or years. Viva Engage, Viva Connections, and most of Teams' core functionality are available. However, some third-party app integrations, certain Power Platform connectors, and specific Graph API endpoints that work in commercial do not function in GCC High. Building your communications and engagement strategy requires knowing which capabilities are available in GCC High before you commit to a platform design — not discovering the gaps after rollout.
Can we use Power Automate and Power Apps to automate workflows in GCC High?
Yes, Power Platform is available in GCC High, but the connector catalog differs significantly from commercial. Many connectors available in commercial Azure are absent or restricted in GCC High. Before building workflows that depend on specific connectors — particularly integrations with external systems — the connector availability needs to be confirmed against the current GCC High connector list. We assess each proposed use case against actual GCC High availability before any build work begins.
How do we handle collaboration with external partners who aren't on GCC High?
B2B guest access in GCC High is more restricted than in commercial Azure AD. External users from commercial Microsoft 365 tenants can be invited as guests in some configurations, but the experience has more friction and limitations than commercial-to-commercial collaboration. Teams Connect (shared channels) availability in GCC High is limited. External collaboration strategy — who can access what, how, and through which channels — needs to be designed up front as part of your governance model, not bolted on after deployment when employees are already working around the restrictions.
How does the platform configuration connect to our CMMC compliance posture?
Directly. Several CMMC control families have requirements that are satisfied or violated at the platform layer: AU (audit logging — Teams and Exchange Online logging configuration), AC (access control — Teams policies and SharePoint external sharing settings), MP (media protection — sensitivity labels and DLP on file sharing), and AT (awareness and training — how you communicate compliance requirements to your workforce). Getting the platform configured correctly is part of getting CMMC right, and a CMMC assessor will ask about your communication platform controls. We configure with both usability and assessor scrutiny in mind.
Do you handle Microsoft licensing?
We assess your current licensing against actual usage and platform requirements, and advise on where you're over-licensed, under-licensed, or paying for capabilities in the wrong tier. We don't resell Microsoft licenses — that runs through your Microsoft relationship or a licensing partner. If you don't have a GCC High licensing partner, we can point you to the right ones.
